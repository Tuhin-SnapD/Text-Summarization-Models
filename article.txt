In 2025, global temperatures remained exceptionally high. NOAA’s National Centers for Environmental Information ranked the year as the third warmest in its records, which extend to 1850. The global surface average was 1.17 degrees Celsius above the twentieth-century average and 1.34 degrees above the 1850–1900 average. The ten warmest years in the record have all occurred since 2015.

Warm conditions affected most of Earth. Europe, Oceania and the Arctic each had their second-warmest year, while every continent and the Arctic ranked among their ten warmest. Land temperatures ranked second highest and ocean temperatures third highest. The strongest warm anomalies occurred in the Arctic, Europe, western and southern Asia, and parts of Antarctica. Some parts of the tropical Pacific and eastern Antarctica were cooler than average, but no land or ocean region recorded an annual cold record.

The upper ocean held a record amount of heat for the fifth year in a row. The ocean stores most of the excess heat in the Earth system, and measurements of the upper 700 metres show a sustained rise since around 1970. Northern Hemisphere snow cover was the third lowest in the record that began in 1967. Arctic sea ice extent was the second lowest since satellite records began in 1979; Antarctic sea ice was the third lowest.

There were 101 named tropical storms worldwide, above the 1991–2020 average of 88. Fifty-two reached tropical-cyclone strength and 24 became major cyclones. Five reached Category 5 strength. Activity differed by ocean basin: several basins had near-average storm counts while others were above average. In December, global temperatures were 1.05 degrees Celsius above the twentieth-century average, making it the fifth-warmest December recorded. The month also had very low sea-ice and snow-cover measurements.

These indicators describe different parts of the climate system. A cooler region or a slower annual increase in one measure does not undo the longer-term warming trend. The figures are annual global summaries and do not describe the conditions experienced in every location.
